## What new design intent did the developers state?

### Takeaway
The strongest new material is Lost Crown's reasoning for capping Memory Shards (save-file size and map clutter), Darkest Dungeon's reversal toward a visible stress meter, Signalis's playstyle-determined endings, and Hi-Fi Rush's "rhythm is optional skill" framing. Few developers described what they cut.

### Cited Findings
- **REPLACED treats fighting as survival, not power fantasy.** "for R.E.A.C.H. fighting is a matter of survival, not something that it enjoys." Arkham was a named influence, "a rhythmic, timing-based dance." [P1] ([Xbox Wire](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2026/04/14/replaced-combat/))
- **REPLACED builds emotional lead-in before boss fights** so players "slam into it, engaged and motivated." [P1] (same page). The page states no cuts.
- **Lost Crown shard limits have two stated reasons:** cloud saves and multiplatform transfer restrict save size, and a cap keeps the map readable. [P2] ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-memory-shards-help-players-find-secrets-in-prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown))
- **Lost Crown iteration:** the maximum never changed; the starting count began at the maximum, dropped to a few, and playtesting set it at 15. Extra shards are chest rewards near blocked paths, which reminds players the feature exists. The stated purpose is reducing cognitive load. [P2] (same page)
- **MIO world intent:** Blumberg says "the story had to co-evolve with the level design," hints sit in the world rather than text tutorials, and many mechanics are deliberately left unexplained. He calls the game "a very non-linear game with a super high skill ceiling." [P2] ([GamePressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/most-challenging-was-letting-go-of-ideas-we-liked-interview-with/z2783))
- **MIO cost of the far camera:** it made NPC emotion hard to express. His hardest lesson: "Most challenging was letting go of ideas we liked." [P2] (same page; no specific cut ideas named)
- **MIO exploration and art:** Hourcade says exploration was the pillar "talked about again and again" and "led to huge changes." The aim was "a 3D world that constantly evoked the sensation of navigating a hand-drawn 2D illustration," with the richest settings kept for after combat or boss arenas. The look uses procedural outlines and watercolour patches. [P2] ([Playday](https://playday.one/2026/01/19/mios-sarah-hourcade-talks-game-pass-inspiration-and-the-games-gorgeous-artstyle))
- **MIO after launch:** the team was "mostly planning on taking a rest." [P2] (same page, January 2026, before the closure reports). No Nacre or modifier statements were on any page I opened.
- **EMMI intent is dread through movement:** "the dread that comes from an inorganic life form nonchalantly chasing after you"; a human-like form on all fours; behaviour that varies, "like creeping behind Samus on two legs." Sakamoto: "I didn't think a normal point of view would cut it." EMMI zones are "Dread Zones," separate from normal areas, and Samus's counterplay is "no different to any other enemy." [P2] ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/metroid-dread-hd-emmi-interview-yoshio-sakamoto/))
- **Darkest Dungeon:** "the ability to fight is only half the equation"; "We want you to have to observe and react to your hero as it happens." Influences were wargame morale, *Aliens* and *The Thing*. Heroes keep an Affliction history and tend to repeat patterns. [P1] ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-darkest-dungeon-s-i-affliction-system))
- **Signalis:** playstyle decides the ending; the team did not intend replays; Stern did not expect many players to reach the early ending, which was meant for players who often don't finish games. [P2] ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/signalis-game-rose-engine-interview))
- **Hi-Fi Rush origin:** "how you love it in a movie trailer when they synchronise the music"; the rhythm layer is something "you don't really need to focus on." Johanas would not pitch until he understood how to use the middleware with the sound data, then built a prototype so Bethesda could play it. [P2] ([Hooked Gamers](https://www.hookedgamers.com/features/2023/10/16/interview_with_john_johanas_%E2%80%93_game_director_of_hi-fi_rush.html)). The GDC page frames the goal as a positive feedback loop that lowers the barrier to entry [P1] ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034256/))
- **UNBEATABLE:** narrative mode exists to "give context to the songs you are playing," so music and action "coalesce into a single thing." Arcade mode introduces the genre with a low floor and high ceiling. [P2] ([Indiecator](https://indiecator.org/2025/08/31/andrew-tsai-unbeatable-interview/)); the page does not say which mechanics serve which story beats.
- **Hollow Knight:** the original concept was a short, replayable game with a random map; the team dropped random generation as "a headache." [P2] ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-the-i-hollow-knight-i-devs-mapped-out-their-metroidvania-))

### Inferences
- Lost Crown's two-reason cap means a DT annotation or memory-pin feature needs a save-size and clutter budget, and the starting count should be tuned in playtests, not set at the maximum.
- Darkest Dungeon's visible meter and Hi-Fi Rush's optional-rhythm stance both point toward legible state. A hidden pair-bond integer is defensible only if tells make its effects readable, which is the author's decision.
- Signalis's playstyle-derived endings, with no replay intent, match DT's logged-but-unseen counters, but both rose-engine quotes are about one-run delivery, so the Wave-Lock chapter structure is a separate question.
- MIO's far camera is the only stated cost of a wide side view; DT's cinematic camera should plan NPC and bond-scene close-ups (inference).

### Gaps
- What developers would change or cut is rarely stated; only Hollow Knight's random map, Lost Crown's starting count and Darkest Dungeon's opacity are documented reversals.
- No developer statement on rhythm timing windows, input buffering or sequences came out of these pages; that belongs to the mechanics track.

### Design questions this raises for DT
1. **Pair-bond integer (Vox-Ciphera bond):** Darkest Dungeon's designers moved from opaque to visible stress. Should the bond stay fully hidden, or surface through a tell the player can read (barks, tone shifts)?
2. **Growth-credit log:** Hi-Fi Rush says rhythm skill should be optional. Should Perfect and Good credits scale growth gently, so a player who is weak at timing is not locked out of progression?
3. **Map and annotation pins (T1.5):** Lost Crown capped shards for save size and clutter and tuned the start count by playtest. What cap and start count does DT's pin system need?
4. **Camera:** MIO's far camera cost NPC emotion. Where does DT's rig cut in for bond and dialogue scenes without breaking the plane lock?
5. **Endings:** Signalis decides endings by overall playstyle and discourages replay. Should DT's Wave-Lock outcomes also derive from accumulated behaviour, and how many distinct endings fit the T1 scope?
6. **Pursuit enemy (EMMI-style):** Sakamoto stresses dread through varied movement and a camera suited to it, in separate zones. Is a scripted chaser worth discussing after T1, given the fourth-enemy cap?
